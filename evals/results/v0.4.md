# v0.4 baseline

The numbers every later sprint is compared with. The summary is in `v0.4.json` next to this file.

- **Run:** 2026-10-21. Opus Tools 0.4.0 was frozen at commit `19b8160` in a git worktree. The cloud run used headless Blender 5.2.2 on Linux, standing in for the user's Windows PC.
- **Suite:** 24 tasks with one run each. The suite hash is `b3208ab30cbd`, from the specs the runs used. v0.4.1 changes the specs, so its hash differs (see the end).
- **Builders:** each task had a fresh Claude Code session that got the `opus eval prompt` text and followed the protocol. The model was not logged in `run_start`, so `models` in the JSON is empty.
- **Graders:** after each builder finished, an independent session graded the run. It re-ran every hard check on copies of the final files, using its own probes and exactly one fresh judge call. Its verdict was logged as a `grade` event, and `accepted` in the summary is that verdict. `self_accepted` is the builder's own accept.
- **Judges:** blind reads and checklists went to fresh headless sessions through `evals/judge.py`. Each judge saw only the images and the questions, never the brief. Blind-read sheets carry no name. Checklist judges saw `look_at` sheets, and in v0.4 their header printed the `.blend` file name (LOOK-2, fixed in v0.4.1). The dragon and chest builders cropped the header, and the goblin builder and grader rendered from a copy named `model.blend`. In other runs, chibi_lowpoly for one, the checklist judge saw the file name, so the soft checklists were not uniformly blind to names.
- **Corrections:** the JSON is summarized from a corrected copy of the ledger. Events that parallel sessions misfiled are moved to their run or dropped where their owner re-logged them, and one rule decides when a round is a hard pass. The section at the end lists every change.
- **Wall-clock is noisy.** Six builder sessions ran at a time on 4 CPUs that other agents also used, so Blender renders and scripts queued. Iteration and rejection counts don't depend on load.
- **Tokens:** summed from each builder's session transcript with `opus eval tokens`. Input counts fresh, cache-write and cache-read tokens. The graders' and judges' tokens are not included.

Reading the JSON:

- 82 entries in `failure_histogram` are `(prose)`. v0.4 accepted free text as findings, and v0.4.1 refuses it (RUN-2). The grader's verdicts are in `grade_failure_histogram`.
- 59 of the 105 validator rejections were `run_log` calls (`run_log` and `run-log` in the histogram), because the help did not list each event's fields. v0.4.1 fixes this (CLI-5, RUN-2).
- The next most rejected tools were `grip_define` (10) and `grip_check` (8). Their causes were axis names, negative values and skinned props (CLI-2, GRIP-1).

## The two failures

- **`human_hd`, blind read.** The builder accepted after its third judge round, where the top guess was "woman character". The grader's single fresh judge said "female character". That is not one of the accepted names (woman, female human, adult woman), so the hard check fails. The other four hard checks pass: lint, height, floating and rig. Across the four fresh blind calls (three by the builder, one by the grader), only two gave an accepted name. The result sits on the boundary of the name list rather than showing a broken model.
- **`peasant_outfit`, poke-through.** The builder abandoned at the 10-round cap after 34.9 minutes. In its last round the probe still measured 8.82 mm of poke-through. The grader measured 13.1 mm as the maximum over the four required poses, in the deep lunge, where the coat front sits inside the raised thigh at the groin. The rest pose alone reaches 6.9 mm, shirt into coat at the armhole. The limit is 1 mm. No v0.4 tool measures garment poke-through, so most of the run went into the builder's own probe, and bugs in that probe cost rounds. Round 9 measured 0.26 mm, but only because hiding the shirt left see-through holes at the neckline. Lint, floating and the blind read ("villager") pass.

## Gate blind spots the graders found

A grader's verdict matched the builder's on 23 of 24 tasks. Graders also found places where a hard check passes without measuring what it claims to measure:

1. **Floating parts on skinned assets.** In v0.4, `model_report` compared only unskinned objects, and it had no `floating_parts` field, so builders read "0" from a missing text line. A planted 8 cm floater on the wolf went unreported. The knight's rerebraces (4.0–4.8 mm) and pauldron lames (about 6 mm), the goblin's tusks (7.5 mm) and a 1.5 cm sliver on the dragon's tail all passed "floating == 0". v0.4.1 fixes this with REP-1, LINT-4 and REP-3 (`report["metrics"]`).
2. **Contacts.** On the chair, a 2.5 cm gap gave no missing-contact line, and a 3 mm gap counted as touching. v0.4.1 caps the contact tolerance at 3 mm and adds `gap_mm` (REP-1).
3. **Penetration gates outside the specs.** `model_report` tagged penetrations as class `hard` with pass false on barrel, chest, round_shield and goblin, and no spec checks them. On the house it reported a false 76.6 mm penetration where two roof bays only abut. v0.4.1 makes penetrations class `report` (REP-2) and treats flush faces as contact (GEOM-1).
4. **Readability measured nothing.** With the spec's arguments, readability ran at the bible's default camera (ue_tp_1080) instead of the task's godot_tp. Defining names that match no part ("hoop", "boss,rim", "trunk") passed with nothing measured. v0.4.1 fixes this with EVAL-1 (camera args, validator rule) and READ-1 (case-insensitive and prefix matching; `not_found` counts as under the floor).
5. **Blind reads.** On the house and dragon, the game-size close and typical frames were taken from inside the subject, or were too tight to read. The judges named the subject from the overview tiles. v0.4.1 flags camera-inside and clipped levels and leaves them off the sheet (CAM-1), and turns the game-size view to the side that shows the most (BLIND-1). The house and dragon specs now pass framing distances. Separately, `judge.py --names` with commas matched nothing, which EVAL-2 fixes.
6. **Lint on shipped glTF.** The glTF importer splits vertices, so pine_trees, forest_valley, crow and wolf_trot exports raised false "faces point inward" errors. v0.4.1 welds imports before linting (LINT-2). Rotated objects also gave false flipped normals, fixed by LINT-1.
7. **Grips.** `grip_check` rejected a prop skinned to the weapon bone (sword_attack) and reported a 59 mm body depth with nothing inside (fp_rifle). v0.4.1 fixes both (GRIP-1, GRIP-2).
8. **Probe checks depend on how the probe is written.** These are not tool bugs. They are for S0b's spec compiler to pin down:
   - plate_armor's "arms raised 150" passes only when 20–40° of the raise goes to the clavicle.
   - fp_rifle's trigger is 2.56 mm or 4.2 mm, depending on what counts as the fingertip pad.
   - wolf_trot's floor passes at frame resolution but fails between frames by 0.07 mm.
   - The skate result changes between a 1 cm and a 2 cm contact threshold.
   - chest's hinge is 0 mm or 22.5 mm, depending on which edge is read.
9. **Soft checklists depend on the renders.** On chest, sword, horse, fp_rifle, forest_valley, dragon, chibi_lowpoly and wolf_trot, the builder's and the grader's judges disagreed. The cause was which views each one rendered, not the model.

## Comparing later sets

v0.4.1 edits the task specs. EVAL-1 adds `camera: godot_tp` to seven readability checks, which changes the suite hash to `16f9b9d9a4be`. The house and dragon blind reads then gain `distances`, which changes it to `c4c1e134168e`. The check ids and accept rules don't change, so `opus eval compare v0.4 <label>` stays per task. It is not like for like everywhere, because some v0.4.1 gates measure more than v0.4's did:

- **Floating parts.** v0.4.1's `model_report` puts skinned meshes and loose islands in the gap graph (REP-1). On the graded v0.4 files, three accepted assets now fail the hard `floating` check: `knight` (5: pauldron lames at 5.9–6.6 mm, rerebraces at 4.6 and 4.9 mm), `goblin` (2: the tusks at 7.5 mm) and `dragon` (1: a tail sliver 15.2 mm off). `peasant_outfit` gets 2, and it was not accepted anyway. The other 14 tasks with a floating check still report 0. A later set may need extra rounds on these three tasks for gaps v0.4 could not see.
- **Readability and blind reads.** The spec changes above, CAM-1 and BLIND-1 change what the readability and blind-read tasks measure.
- **Run rules.** v0.4.1 refuses a round with zero hard failures that lists findings, a grade on an open run, and an event without a run id while another run is open. The corrected baseline above follows the same rules.
